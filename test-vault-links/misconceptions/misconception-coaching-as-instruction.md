# Coaching as Instruction

## Misconception
The misconception is that coaching is a form of instruction, teaching, or tuition where the coach provides technical input, direction, and answers to improve a person's performance.

## Why it happens
This misconception arises from common dictionary definitions and traditional professional practices. The source states that typical definitions of "coaching" include "sports instruction or training, private tuition, and extra teaching." Furthermore, when Timothy Gallwey introduced his methods, professional coaches and instructors felt threatened because they believed he was undermining the established principles of teaching sport, which were based on their authority and technical instruction.

## Correction
Coaching is not instruction. According to the source, coaching is "unlocking people's potential to maximize their own performance." It is a process of helping people learn by discovering from within, facilitated by a powerful working relationship and communication style. It focuses on reducing internal interference to allow natural ability and learning to emerge, rather than providing technical input or teaching. The core equation, Performance = potential – interference, summarizes that coaching improves performance by growing potential and decreasing interference, not by direct instruction.
