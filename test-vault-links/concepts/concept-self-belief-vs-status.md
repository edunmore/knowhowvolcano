# Self-Belief (vs. Status)

## Definition
Self-belief is not explicitly defined as a concept in the source text. It is mentioned in passing as a quality that can be undermined by traditional, instruction-based teaching methods. The text contrasts this with the coach's "authority" or "status," which is tied to their role as an instructor and knowledge-holder.

## Operationalization
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) How can one observe the presence or absence of self-belief in a learner? (2) What specific behaviors or outcomes indicate strong self-belief versus reliance on external authority?

## Boundary conditions
The source implies that self-belief is relevant in learning and performance contexts, particularly when the goal is to unlock a person's natural potential. It suggests that traditional teaching methods, which emphasize the instructor's status and authority, can interfere with the development of self-belief. The concept may not apply in contexts where rote instruction is the sole objective.
