# Acorn Analogy for Human Potential

## Definition
The Acorn Analogy for Human Potential is a psychological model that posits humans are not empty vessels to be filled with knowledge, but are instead like acorns, each containing the inherent potential to become a magnificent oak tree. This potential is realized through nourishment, encouragement, and the presence of light to reach toward, rather than through direct instruction.

## Operationalization
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) What specific practices constitute "nourishment" and "encouragement" in a human development context? (2) How does one identify and support the "tap root" of an individual's potential?

## Boundary conditions
The source implies the model applies to human learning and development, challenging old instructional methods. It notes that "habits die hard and old methods persist," suggesting the model's application may be hindered by entrenched teaching habits and a lack of awareness of the "tap root" (the core, inherent potential). The source does not specify explicit limits or conditions where the model fails.
