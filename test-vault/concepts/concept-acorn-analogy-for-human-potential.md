# Acorn Analogy for Human Potential

## Definition
A psychological model that compares human beings to acorns, positing that each person inherently contains the full potential for growth and development, akin to an acorn containing the potential to become an oak tree. This potential is realized not through instruction but through providing the necessary conditions of nourishment, encouragement, and light.

## Operationalization
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) What observable behaviors in a learner or coachee correspond to the healthy development of their "tap root"? (2) How can a coach or leader practically provide the "nourishment, encouragement, and light" described in the analogy?

## Boundary conditions
The analogy is grounded in an optimistic psychological model that contrasts with older behaviorist views. It is implied that the analogy is less applicable or harder to implement within systems reliant on traditional, instructional teaching methods, where "habits die hard and old methods persist." The source also notes that the analogy's success was historically hard to demonstrate because few used the methods fully.
