# Mentoring (vs. Coaching)

## Definition
Mentoring is a developmental relationship where a more experienced person passes down their knowledge and expertise to a less experienced person, often framed as "tell[ing] all you know." This is distinct from coaching, which does not depend on subject-matter expertise but on coaching expertise to build self-belief and sustained performance from the coachee's own experience.

## Operationalization
Mentoring can be observed when a senior figure (like Mentor with Telemachus) is entrusted with the education of a protégé and imparts their own knowledge and technical skills. In a business context, it is operationalized by the mentor sharing their experience and technical know-how, as when a rowing coach teaches athletes "all [they] knew technically."

## Boundary conditions
Mentoring applies when the primary goal is knowledge transfer from an expert to a novice. It fails or is limited when the learner's potential is constrained by the mentor's own knowledge, as the original myth "unwittingly set some limits to mentoring." It does not apply when the aim is to build self-belief and sustained performance through the learner's own perceptions, which is the domain of coaching.
