# Spend Friction Where It Buys Transfer

## Rule
When designing learning, do not aim to eliminate all [[Friction Budget|friction]]; instead, intentionally invest learner effort only in activities that demonstrably improve [[Transfer|transfer]] to real-world, high-pressure contexts.

## Rationale
Every learning unit has a limited [[Friction Budget]] of mental effort a learner will spend before quitting or guessing. Spending this budget on superficial content (e.g., extra theory, multiple examples) raises friction without improving the ability to perform under stress. Effective learning design uses the budget on deliberate practice elements—like making choices under [[Constraint|constraints]], receiving [[Two-Speed Feedback]], and engaging in the [[Calibration Loop]]—that directly build the skill needed for higher rungs of the [[Outcome Ladder]], especially [[Rung D: Transfer]].
