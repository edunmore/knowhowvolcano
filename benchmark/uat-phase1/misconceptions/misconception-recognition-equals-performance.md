# Recognition Equals Performance

## Misconception
The false belief that if a learner can recognize or understand a concept in a training context (e.g., nodding at a good model), they will be able to perform the associated behavior in a real-world situation.

## Why it happens
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) Is this misconception driven by the ease of assessing [[Recognition (Rung B)]] versus [[Performance (Rung C)]]? (2) Does it stem from a misunderstanding of the role of stress and context in [[Transfer (Rung D)]]?

## Correction
[[Recognition]] is not [[Performance]]. True [[Transfer]] requires [[Spaced Repetition]] over time and deliberate [[Practice Under Constraints]] to overcome [[Interference Under Stress]]. Learning design must target higher rungs of the [[Outcome Ladder]] to achieve performance.
