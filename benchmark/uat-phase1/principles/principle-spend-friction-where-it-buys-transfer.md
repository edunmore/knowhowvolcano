# Spend Friction Where It Buys Transfer

## Rule
When designing a learning unit, do not simply minimize [[Cognitive Load|friction]]; instead, strategically allocate the learner's limited [[Friction Budget]] to activities that directly build [[Transfer (Learning)|transfer]] to real-world performance under stress.

## Rationale
Every learning unit has a finite [[Friction Budget]]; exceeding it causes learners to quit or resort to superficial reading. However, making everything easy does not lead to [[Performance (Rung C)]] or [[Transfer (Rung D)]]. Friction is a resource that should be invested in deliberate practice elements—like making choices under realistic [[Scenario Constraints]] and receiving [[Two-Speed Feedback]]—that help learners bridge the gap between recognition and reliable application in different contexts. This counters the common misconception that "more content means more learning," which often adds friction without improving transfer.
