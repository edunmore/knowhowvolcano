# Outcome Ladder

## Definition
The Outcome Ladder is a framework for categorizing learning outcomes into four ascending levels of competence, from simple recall to reliable application in challenging situations. It separates outcomes into four rungs: Remember (A), Recognize (B), Perform (C), and Transfer (D). This model is used to design learning that targets real-world performance rather than just knowledge recognition.

## Operationalization
The ladder is operationalized by its four distinct rungs: Rung A (remember) is the ability to recall terms; Rung B (recognize) is the ability to pick the right option from examples; Rung C (perform) is the ability to do the behavior in a realistic scenario; and Rung D (transfer) is the ability to do it later, in a different context, under stress. An "observable pass condition" can be written to measure achievement for a chosen rung. A common mistake is to test only Rung A and celebrate progress while performance stays flat.

## Boundary conditions
The toolbox that includes the Outcome Ladder works best when the skill can be observed, even roughly. It can fail when outcomes are invisible or when the environment blocks practice (e.g., no permission to try, high risk, or strict scripts).
