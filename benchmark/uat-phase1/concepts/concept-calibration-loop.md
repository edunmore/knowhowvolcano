# Calibration Loop

## Definition
The **Calibration Loop** is a learning process where a learner improves their judgment by comparing their own choice or decision to an expert's choice and understanding the reason for any difference. Calibration happens fastest when the learner must commit to a decision before seeing the answer and when the feedback highlights the key variable (e.g., risk, trust, time, power, or uncertainty) that influenced the expert's judgment.

## Operationalization
Calibration is observed and measured within a structured practice loop, such as the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]]. It is operationalized by:
1.  Presenting a learner with a realistic scenario and a constrained choice (e.g., between two actions).
2.  Requiring the learner to commit to a choice and provide a one-sentence reason.
3.  Providing immediate [[Two-Speed Feedback]] that reveals the expert's choice and explicitly names the "hidden variable" or key principle (e.g., "skipping the diagnostic step") that distinguishes the correct from the incorrect action.

## Boundary conditions
The calibration process is most effective for skills that are observable and can be practiced. According to the source, it "can fail when outcomes are invisible or when the environment blocks practice (for example: no permission to try, high risk, or strict scripts)." The source also implies calibration is critical for achieving [[Transfer (Learning Outcome)]] beyond simple recognition.
