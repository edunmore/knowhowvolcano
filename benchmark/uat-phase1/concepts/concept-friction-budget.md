# Friction Budget

## Definition
The **Friction Budget** is the maximum amount of mental effort a learner can spend on a learning unit before they disengage by quitting or resorting to superficial guessing. It is a finite resource that is consumed when a unit is overly complex, requiring too many new terms, steps, or context switches.

## Operationalization
Friction can be identified by observing learner behaviors such as abandoning a task or defaulting to "superficial reading" instead of [[Deliberate Practice]]. It grows when a unit presents "too many new terms at once, too many steps, or too much switching between screens." The budget is exceeded when these conditions cause learners to stop engaging with the material meaningfully.

## Boundary Conditions
The concept applies to the design of [[Microlearning]] units aimed at skill transfer. It is not about making everything easy, but about strategically "spend[ing] friction where it buys transfer." An open question is how to adapt it for expert learners who may become bored with low-friction content. The source also questions its applicability when "outcomes are invisible or when the environment blocks practice."
