# Manager Scenario: Missed Deadlines

## Scenario
A manager is presented with a scenario where a team member says, "I keep missing deadlines because other teams block me." The manager must choose between two actions to respond.

## Context
This is a sample scenario used within a microlearning unit designed for new managers. The goal is to help them develop skills for real-world conversations. The scenario is part of the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]], which includes creating a short scenario with a constraint (like an annoyed colleague) and offering two actions: an intended one and a tempting wrong one.

## Key Insight
The scenario illustrates the common mistake of jumping to advice-giving (Action B) instead of first engaging in diagnostic questioning (Action A). It is used to teach the importance of the diagnostic step to avoid triggering defensiveness and to ensure the solution fits the real constraints. This aligns with the principle that [[Recognition is Not Performance]] and that learning must target higher rungs on the [[Outcome Ladder]] like performance and [[Transfer]].

## Structure
- **Setup:** A team member reports a recurring problem of missed deadlines, blaming other teams.
- **Tension:** The manager must choose a response under the implicit pressure to be helpful.
- **Resolution:** The learner must commit to either Action A (ask for a concrete example and explore constraints) or Action B (give immediate advice).
- **Outcome:** The example demonstrates that Action B, while tempting, often skips diagnosis and can cause defensiveness, whereas Action A is the intended, more effective managerial behavior.
