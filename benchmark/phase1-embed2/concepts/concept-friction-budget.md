# Friction Budget

## Definition
The Friction Budget is the maximum amount of mental effort a learner can expend on a learning unit before they disengage or resort to superficial strategies like guessing. It is a design constraint that emphasizes allocating cognitive load strategically to promote skill transfer rather than minimizing difficulty indiscriminately.

## Key Components
Key factors that increase friction, as described in the source, include:
- **Excessive Novelty**: Introducing too many new terms or concepts at once.
- **Procedural Complexity**: Requiring too many steps to complete a task.
- **Interface Switching**: Forcing the learner to switch between multiple screens or contexts unnecessarily.

## Application
In learning design, the Friction Budget is applied by carefully managing the cognitive demands of a unit. The goal is not to eliminate all difficulty but to "spend friction where it buys transfer." Exceeding the budget causes learners to "stop doing deliberate practice and default to superficial reading." Designers must balance challenge with manageability to maintain engagement and promote deep learning.
