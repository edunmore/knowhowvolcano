# Spend Friction Where It Buys Transfer

## Rule
When designing learning, do not avoid all difficulty; instead, strategically allocate the learner's mental effort (friction) to activities that directly build the ability to apply skills in new, real-world contexts (transfer).

## Rationale
Every learning unit has a finite "Friction Budget"—the mental effort a learner can expend before disengaging. Exceeding this budget causes learners to default to superficial reading. However, the goal is not to eliminate all friction, as some is necessary for deep learning. The principle states that friction should be invested specifically in tasks that promote transfer, such as practicing with realistic constraints, rather than on extraneous elements like absorbing excessive new terminology or navigating complex interfaces.
