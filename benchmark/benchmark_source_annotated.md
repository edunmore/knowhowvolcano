## Context
A team wants to turn internal know-how into short learning units for new managers. They keep producing “nice-to-read” pages, but managers still fail in real conversations. The team assumes the problem is motivation. A coach argues the real problem is [[Interference]]: people freeze under pressure, forget the first step, or choose the wrong tool because they misunderstand what the tool is for.

## Concept 1 — [[Outcome Ladder]]
The Outcome Ladder separates outcomes into four rungs: remember, recognize, perform, transfer.

## Principle 1 — [[Friction Budget]]
Every learning unit has a friction budget: the maximum mental effort a learner can spend before they quit or start guessing.

## Concept 2 — [[Two-Speed Feedback]]
Feedback should come in two speeds: a fast [[Rule of Thumb]] and a slow explanation including boundary conditions.

## Misconception 1 — [[More content means more learning]]
Extra content can raise [[Friction Budget]] without improving [[Transfer]].

## Misconception 2 — [[If they understood it once, they will perform it later]]
[[Recognition]] is not [[Performance]]. Transfer requires [[Spaced Repetition]] and practice under constraints.

## Concept 3 — [[Calibration Loop]]
Calibration improves fastest when the learner commits to a decision and feedback names the hidden variable (risk, trust, time, power, uncertainty).
