# Recognition Equals Performance

## Misconception
The wrong belief that if a learner can recognize or understand a concept or model once, they will be able to perform the associated behavior reliably later in a real-world situation. This is exemplified by the mistaken assumption that testing only [[Outcome Ladder]] Rung A (recognition/recall) and celebrating progress is sufficient, even when actual performance remains flat.

## Why it happens
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) Is this misconception driven by an over-reliance on simple, low-friction assessment methods? (2) Does it stem from confusing immediate comprehension with long-term skill retention?

## Correction
Recognition is not performance. True skill development requires progression to higher rungs of the [[Outcome Ladder]], specifically to Rung C (perform) and Rung D (transfer). Achieving [[Transfer]] requires "repetition spaced over time and practice under constraints," such as time pressure or stress, as outlined in the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]] and [[Calibration Loop]].
