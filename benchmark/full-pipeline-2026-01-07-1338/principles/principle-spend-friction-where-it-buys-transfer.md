# Spend Friction Where It Buys Transfer

## Rule
When designing learning, deliberately direct the learner's limited [[Friction Budget]] towards activities that are necessary for achieving [[Transfer]].

## Rationale
In learning, exceeding the [[Friction Budget]] causes learners to quit deliberate practice. However, the goal is not to eliminate friction entirely, but to invest it where it yields the highest outcome, specifically the ability to perform in new contexts and under pressure. The source states that "transfer requires repetition spaced over time and practice under constraints," indicating where valuable friction should be applied.
