# Friction Budget

## Definition
The Friction Budget is a concept in [[Microlearning Design]] that refers to the maximum amount of mental effort a learner can invest in a learning unit before they disengage or resort to superficial strategies like guessing. It is not about eliminating difficulty, but about strategically allocating cognitive load to purchase meaningful skill [[Transfer]].

## Operationalization
Friction can be observed to increase when a learning unit introduces too many [[New Terms]] at once, requires too many procedural steps, or demands excessive switching between screens or contexts. Exceeding the budget is signaled by learners abandoning [[Deliberate Practice]] and defaulting to passive reading. The budget is managed by designing units within the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]], which constrains complexity to target a specific rung on the [[Outcome Ladder]].

## Boundary conditions
The concept applies to the design of discrete [[Microlearning]] units intended to build performance. It does not imply that all friction is bad; the principle is to "spend friction where it buys transfer." The budget may not apply to learning activities where the goal is purely exploration or knowledge acquisition without an immediate performance target.
