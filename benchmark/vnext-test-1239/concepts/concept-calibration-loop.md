# Calibration Loop

## Definition
The Calibration Loop is a learning process where a learner improves their judgment by comparing their own decision to an expert's decision and understanding the difference. This comparison is most effective when the learner must commit to a choice before seeing the answer and when the feedback highlights a specific, hidden variable influencing the decision.

## Operationalization
Calibration is observed and facilitated within a structured practice unit, like a [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]]. The learner is presented with a short scenario and forced to choose between actions (e.g., an intended action and a tempting wrong action) and provide a reason. After committing, they receive [[Two-Speed Feedback]] that explicitly names the "hidden variable" (e.g., risk, trust, time, power, or uncertainty) that explains the discrepancy between their choice and the expert choice.

## Boundary conditions
The Calibration Loop is best applied to skills that can be observed, even roughly. It can fail when outcomes are invisible or when the learner's environment blocks practice (e.g., no permission to try, high risk, or strict scripts). Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) How does calibration differ for expert vs. novice learners? (2) What specific feedback structures optimize calibration for complex, multi-variable decisions?
