# Managerial Coaching Scenario

## Scenario
A manager is presented with a coaching opportunity. A team member states, “I keep missing deadlines because other teams block me.” The manager must choose between two coaching actions: taking time to diagnose the problem or giving immediate advice.

## Context
The scenario is presented as a sample for a coaching [[Toolbox]], intended to illustrate how to practice a skill. The implied context is a typical workplace where a manager must respond to a subordinate's recurring problem. The source text suggests the scenario is designed for practice, where the learner (the manager) must make a choice before receiving feedback.

## Key Insight
The example illustrates that a common, intuitive managerial response—giving immediate advice—can be a mistake because it skips the essential [[Diagnostic Step]] and can trigger defensiveness. Effective coaching under [[Time Pressure]] requires resisting the urge to be immediately helpful in favor of first understanding the problem.

## Structure
- **Setup:** A team member reports a recurring issue of missed deadlines due to being blocked by other teams.
- **Tension:** The manager must choose a response under the implicit pressure of the moment.
- **Resolution:** The source text presents two possible actions:
    - **Action A:** "Ask for a concrete recent example and explore constraints before proposing a solution."
    - **Action B:** "Give advice immediately: 'Just communicate earlier and set boundaries.'"
- **Outcome:** The text states that many new managers choose the tempting but often wrong Action B, which fails to diagnose the real issue and risks causing defensiveness.

## Rewrite Opportunity
- **Core Pattern:** The pattern is a forced-choice exercise between a patient, diagnostic approach and a quick, prescriptive one, designed to reveal a common [[Cognitive Bias]] in managerial problem-solving.
- **Transferable Elements:** This structure can be adapted to any domain where an expert (coach, manager, mentor) must respond to a problem report. The key transferable element is the inclusion of a "tempting wrong action" that feels helpful but is suboptimal, forcing the learner to [[Calibrate]] their judgment.
