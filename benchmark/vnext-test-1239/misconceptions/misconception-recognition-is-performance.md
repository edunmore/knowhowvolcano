# Recognition Is Performance

## Misconception
The mistaken belief that understanding or agreeing with a good model or concept (nodding in recognition) is equivalent to being able to apply it effectively in real situations. It is the assumption that "If they understood it once, they will perform it later."

## Why it happens
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) Is it driven by an over-reliance on [[Cognitive Load]] theory at the expense of [[Transfer]]? (2) Does it stem from confusing [[Recognition]] with the ability to execute under pressure?

## Correction
[[Recognition]] is not [[Performance]]. True skill [[Transfer]] requires [[Spaced Repetition]] over time and [[Deliberate Practice]] under realistic constraints (like time pressure or an annoyed colleague), not just intellectual assent. Effective learning design must bridge this gap using tools like the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]] and [[Two-Speed Feedback]].
