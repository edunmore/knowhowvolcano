# More Content Means More Learning

## Misconception
The mistaken belief that adding more examples, theory, or content to a learning unit inherently increases the amount of learning that occurs.

## Why it happens
The source text does not specify the underlying causes for why teams hold this belief. Open questions: (1) Is it a confusion between the quantity of information and the quality of understanding? (2) Does it stem from a traditional, content-centric model of education?

## Correction
In reality, extra content can raise [[Friction Budget|friction]] without improving [[Transfer (Outcome Ladder)]] of the skill. Effective learning design prioritizes spending friction where it "buys transfer." If a learning unit requires lengthy justification for a [[Rule of Thumb|rule of thumb]], it is likely targeting the wrong rung of the [[Outcome Ladder]] or mixing multiple skills, thereby overwhelming the learner.
