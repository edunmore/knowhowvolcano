# Spend Friction Where It Buys Transfer

## Rule
Do not aim to make all learning easy. Instead, allocate the learner's limited [[Friction Budget]] to demands that directly improve [[Transfer (Learning Outcome)]], such as practicing under realistic [[Constraint (Learning Design)]] like time pressure or emotional tension.

## Rationale
Every [[Microlearning]] unit has a finite capacity for learner effort before they disengage. If this budget is spent on extraneous details, like too many new terms or complex navigation, learners default to superficial reading. However, friction is necessary for achieving higher rungs on the [[Outcome Ladder]], particularly the ability to perform under stress. Therefore, friction should be strategically invested in activities that force calibration, deliberate practice, and application in varied contexts, as these are what build durable skill.
