# Friction Budget

## Rule
Every learning unit has a finite "friction budget," which is the maximum mental effort a learner can expend before they quit or resort to superficial guessing. This budget should be spent deliberately to "buy transfer" to real-world performance, not on extraneous content.

## Rationale
Exceeding the friction budget causes learners to stop engaging in deliberate practice and default to passive reading. Friction increases when a unit introduces too many new terms, too many steps, or requires too much switching between contexts. The principle is not about making everything easy, but about allocating limited learner effort to where it yields the highest return in skill application and transfer.
