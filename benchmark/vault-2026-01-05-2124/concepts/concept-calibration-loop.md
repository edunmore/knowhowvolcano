# Calibration Loop

## Definition
A process where a learner improves their judgment by comparing their own decision to an expert's choice and understanding the difference. It is accelerated when the learner commits to a decision before seeing the answer and when the feedback highlights a hidden contextual variable.

## Operationalization
The process can be observed in a structured learning activity where a learner is presented with a scenario and two possible actions. The learner must first commit to a choice and provide a brief reason. They then receive feedback that contrasts their choice with the expert choice and explicitly names a hidden variable (e.g., risk, trust, time) that explains the difference.

## Boundary conditions
The effectiveness of calibration is implied to depend on the learning environment. The source states the broader toolbox for skill development works best when the skill can be observed and can fail when outcomes are invisible or when the environment blocks practice (e.g., no permission to try, high risk). It is reasonable to infer these as boundary conditions for the Calibration Loop as well.
