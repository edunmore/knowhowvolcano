# Calibration Loop

## Definition
A [[Calibration Loop]] is a learning process where a learner improves their judgment by comparing their own decision to an expert's choice and understanding the difference. Calibration occurs most effectively when the learner must commit to a decision before seeing the answer and when the feedback explicitly identifies the critical hidden variable (e.g., risk, trust, time, power, or uncertainty) that influenced the expert's judgment.

## Operationalization
The process can be observed within a structured learning unit, such as the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]]. The learner is presented with a scenario and must choose between two actions. After making a committed choice, they receive feedback that provides a fast, memorable rule of thumb and a deeper explanation that names the hidden variable. The improvement in calibration is measured by the learner's increasing ability to correctly identify and apply the relevant hidden variables in subsequent decisions.

## Boundary conditions
The source text implies that the [[Calibration Loop]] works best for skills that are observable and can be practiced. It can fail "when outcomes are invisible or when the environment blocks practice (for example: no permission to try, high risk, or strict scripts)." The technique is designed to build [[Transfer]] of skills, which requires repetition spaced over time and practice under constraints, not just one-time recognition.
