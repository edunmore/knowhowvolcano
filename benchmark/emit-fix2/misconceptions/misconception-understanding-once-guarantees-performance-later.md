# Understanding Once Guarantees Performance Later

## Misconception
The belief that if a learner understands or can recall a concept once ([[Recognition]]), they will automatically be able to perform the associated skill later in a real-world context ([[Performance]] and [[Transfer]]). This leads to the design of learning that only targets recall and comprehension, mistakenly equating it with behavioral change.

## Why it happens
The source text implies this happens because teams confuse different levels of learning outcome, as defined by the [[Outcome Ladder]]. A common mistake is to "test only Rung A and celebrate progress while performance stays flat." It also stems from underestimating the effects of [[Interference]], such as stress or time pressure, which can cause learners to forget or choose the wrong action despite prior understanding.

## Correction
[[Recognition Is Not Performance]]. To achieve reliable performance later, learning must target higher rungs on the [[Outcome Ladder]], specifically Rung C ([[Perform]]) and Rung D ([[Transfer]]). This requires deliberate practice under realistic constraints and "repetition spaced over time." Effective learning design, such as the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]], incorporates scenarios with constraints and requires learners to commit to a decision, facilitating the [[Calibration Loop]] necessary for transfer.
