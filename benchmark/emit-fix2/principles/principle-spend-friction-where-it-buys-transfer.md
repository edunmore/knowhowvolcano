# Spend Friction Where It Buys Transfer

## Rule
When designing a learning unit, deliberately allocate the learner's limited [[Friction Budget]] to elements that directly enable [[Transfer]], rather than wasting it on unnecessary complexity or extraneous content.

## Rationale
Every learner has a limited [[Friction Budget]]—a maximum amount of mental effort they will expend before quitting or resorting to superficial reading. The principle states that not all friction is bad; the goal is not to make everything easy, but to invest friction strategically in activities that promote [[Transfer]] (Rung D on the [[Outcome Ladder]]). This counters the common misconception that "more content means more learning," which often raises friction without improving transfer. Effective design, such as the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]], uses friction to create realistic practice under constraints, which is essential for performance later, in different contexts, and under stress.
