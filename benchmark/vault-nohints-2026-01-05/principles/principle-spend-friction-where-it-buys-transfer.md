# Spend Friction Where It Buys Transfer

## Rule
When designing learning, do not eliminate all friction. Instead, strategically allocate mental effort ("friction") only to elements that directly contribute to the learner's ability to transfer a skill to a real, stressful context later.

## Rationale
Every learning unit has a finite "friction budget" before learners disengage or guess. Spending this budget on unnecessary content (like extra theory or examples) can raise friction without improving transfer. The principle argues that the goal is not to make everything easy, but to ensure the required effort is invested in activities that build durable, applicable performance (Rung D of the Outcome Ladder), such as making choices under constraints and receiving calibrated feedback.
