# Manager Scenario: Missed Deadlines

## Scenario
A team member tells their manager, "I keep missing deadlines because other teams block me." The manager is presented with two potential actions: one involves asking for a concrete example to explore constraints before proposing a solution, and the other involves giving immediate advice to communicate earlier and set boundaries.

## Context
This scenario appears as a sample within a chapter about designing effective microlearning for managers. The chapter's context describes a team trying to turn internal know-how into learning units but finding that managers still fail in real conversations due to interference (freezing under pressure, forgetting steps, or misusing tools).

## Key Insight
The example illustrates the "7-Minute Microlearning Loop" design principle, specifically the step of creating a short scenario with a constraint (like an annoyed colleague or missing data) and offering two actions: the intended action and a tempting wrong action. It serves as a test of performance (Outcome Ladder Rung C) rather than just recall or recognition.

## Structure
- **Setup:** A manager is confronted with a team member's complaint about missed deadlines due to external blockers.
- **Tension:** The manager must choose how to respond under the implicit pressure of the situation.
- **Resolution:** The scenario presents two distinct choices, forcing a deliberate decision.
- **Outcome:** The intended action (Action A) models a coaching approach focused on understanding before advising, which is the target behavior for learning transfer.

## Rewrite Opportunity
- **Core Pattern:** A short, constrained decision point that pits a deliberate, inquiry-based approach against a reactive, advice-giving one.
- **Transferable Elements:** This pattern can be adapted to any domain where a learner must choose between a surface-level fix and a deeper diagnostic action under pressure (e.g., customer service, technical troubleshooting, conflict mediation).
