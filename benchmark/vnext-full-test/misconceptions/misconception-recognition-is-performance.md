# Recognition Is Performance

## Misconception
The mistaken belief that if a learner can recognize or understand a concept or model (i.e., "nod at a good model"), they will be able to perform the related behavior later in a real-world situation.

## Why it happens
The source text implies this misconception arises from a flawed [[Learning Design]] approach that conflates different levels of competency. It is a common mistake to test only for recall ([[Rung A]]) or recognition ([[Rung B]]) on the [[Outcome Ladder]] and celebrate this as progress, while actual performance remains flat.

## Correction
Recognition is not performance. True [[Transfer (Learning)]] requires [[Deliberate Practice]] under constraints (like time pressure or stress) and [[Spaced Repetition]] over time. Learning design must target higher rungs ([[Rung C]] and [[Rung D]]) to build performance capability.
