# Friction Budget

## Definition
The **Friction Budget** is a concept representing the maximum amount of mental effort a learner can expend on a learning unit before they disengage or resort to superficial tactics like guessing. It is a finite resource that is consumed by demands such as processing too many new terms, steps, or screen switches at once.

## Operationalization
Friction is observed or identified when a learning unit requires "too many new terms at once, too many steps, or too much switching between screens." The key indicator that the budget is exceeded is a change in learner behavior: they "stop doing [[Deliberate Practice]] and default to superficial reading." The concept is operationalized during the design of a [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]] to ensure friction is spent strategically to "buy transfer."

## Boundary conditions
The concept applies to the design of learning units, particularly [[Microlearning]]. It does not mean "make everything easy." Instead, it is a principle for allocating cognitive load efficiently toward achieving [[Transfer (Learning)]] on the [[Outcome Ladder]]. Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) How is the specific budget quantified or estimated for a given learner? (2) Does the budget vary based on the target rung of the [[Outcome Ladder]]?
