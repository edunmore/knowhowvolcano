# Manager Scenario: Missing Deadlines

## Scenario
A team member tells their manager, “I keep missing deadlines because other teams block me.” The manager is presented with a choice between two possible actions: Action A (ask for a concrete recent example) and an unspecified second, "tempting wrong action." This scenario is used as a sample within the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]].

## Context
The scenario is presented as a realistic constraint for a new manager to practice within a [[Microlearning]] unit. It is designed to create [[Transfer (Learning)]] by simulating a common managerial pressure point. The scenario is part of a learning loop intended to help managers avoid [[Interference (Learning)]]—such as freezing under pressure—and to calibrate their decision-making.

## Key Insight
This example illustrates how to design a [[Microlearning]] unit that targets higher rungs of the [[Outcome Ladder]], specifically [[Rung C: Perform]] or [[Rung D: Transfer]], by placing the learner in a constrained, realistic scenario with a choice between a correct and a tempting incorrect action. It embodies the principle that [[Recognition Is Not Performance]].

## Structure
- **Setup:** A manager is confronted by a team member who is consistently missing deadlines and blaming other teams.
- **Tension:** The manager must choose how to respond effectively under the implicit pressure of the situation.
- **Resolution:** The source text cuts off before revealing the full resolution, but the structure implies the learner must choose between Action A and another action.
- **Outcome:** The intended outcome is for the learner to practice applying a correct managerial response and receive [[Two-Speed Feedback]] to improve [[Calibration]].

## Rewrite Opportunity
- **Core Pattern:** A subordinate presents a performance problem with an external attribution ("other teams block me"); the manager's task is to guide the conversation toward concrete details and shared problem-solving.
- **Transferable Elements:** This pattern can be adapted to any coaching or managerial conversation where an individual deflects responsibility. The key transferable element is the constraint of a "tempting wrong action" (e.g., immediately offering a solution or agreeing with the blame) versus the productive action of probing for specifics.
