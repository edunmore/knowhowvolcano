# More Content Means More Learning

## Misconception
The wrong belief that adding more examples, theory, or explanatory content to a learning unit inherently increases the amount of learning. Teams often hold this belief, thinking more content leads to better outcomes.

## Why it happens
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) What organizational or cultural factors reinforce this belief? (2) Is it driven by a confusion between information delivery and skill [[Transfer (Learning)]]?

## Correction
In reality, extra content can raise [[Friction Budget|friction]] without improving [[Transfer (Learning)]]. If a unit requires lengthy justification (e.g., three paragraphs for a [[Rule Of Thumb]]), it is likely targeting the wrong rung of the [[Outcome Ladder]] or mixing multiple skills. Effective design focuses on spending friction where it directly buys transfer, not on content volume.
