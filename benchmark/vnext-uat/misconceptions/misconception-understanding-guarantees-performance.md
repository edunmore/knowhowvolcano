# Understanding Guarantees Performance

## Misconception
The mistaken belief that if a learner understands or can recall a concept or model ([[Rung A]]) once, they will be able to perform the related skill in a real-world, high-pressure situation later on.

## Why it happens
The source text suggests this misconception arises from a confusion between different levels of learning, particularly between [[Recognition]] and [[Performance]]. Designers may mistakenly believe that adding more content or theory increases learning, and they may celebrate progress on recall-based tests while real-world performance remains unchanged. This is a common mistake in designing learning units.

## Correction
Understanding or recall does not guarantee [[Transfer]] or [[Performance]]. To achieve performance under real-world constraints ([[Rung C]] and [[Rung D]]), learners need [[Deliberate Practice]] under [[Constraint|Constraints]] (like time pressure or stress) and [[Spaced Repetition]] over time. Effective learning design must target higher rungs on the [[Outcome Ladder]] and use tools like the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]] to build performance through practice and [[Calibration Loop|calibration]].
