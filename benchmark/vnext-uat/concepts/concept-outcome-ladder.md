# Outcome Ladder

## Definition
The **Outcome Ladder** is a framework for classifying learning outcomes into four distinct levels or "rungs." It helps clarify what true success looks like in a real-world context by distinguishing between the ability to recall information and the ability to apply skills under realistic pressure. It is introduced as part of a toolbox for designing effective [[Microlearning]] units.

## Operationalization
The ladder is operationalized by its four rungs, which represent observable levels of capability:
- **Rung A: Remember** – The learner can recall terms.
- **Rung B: Recognize** – The learner can pick the right option from examples.
- **Rung C: Perform** – The learner can do the behavior in a realistic scenario.
- **Rung D: Transfer** – The learner can do it later, in a different context, under stress.
A key application is selecting a target rung as the first step in the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]].

## Boundary conditions
The framework is presented specifically within the context of designing [[Microlearning]] to combat *interference*—where learners fail in real situations due to pressure or misunderstanding. Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) Is the ladder intended to be a linear progression, or can rungs be targeted independently? (2) Does it apply equally to all types of skills and knowledge domains, or is it optimized for procedural/behavioral skills?
