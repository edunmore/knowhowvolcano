# Friction Budget

## Definition
The **Friction Budget** is the maximum amount of mental effort a learner can expend on a learning unit before they disengage or resort to superficial tactics like guessing. It is a finite resource that is consumed when a unit demands too many new terms, steps, or cognitive switches at once. The principle is not to eliminate all friction, but to spend it strategically to buy [[Transfer]].

## Operationalization
Friction can be observed when a learning unit requires "too many new terms at once, too many steps, or too much switching between screens." When the budget is exceeded, learners "stop doing [[Deliberate Practice]] and default to superficial reading." Therefore, it is measured indirectly by monitoring learner behavior for signs of quitting or shallow engagement, and directly by analyzing the unit's design for excessive cognitive load elements.

## Boundary conditions
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) Does the friction budget vary per individual learner or is it a general design constraint? (2) Is there a way to quantitatively estimate a unit's friction cost before deployment?
