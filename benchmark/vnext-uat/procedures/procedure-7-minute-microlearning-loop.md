# 7-Minute Microlearning Loop

## When to use
Use this loop to design a single microlearning unit, particularly when turning internal know-how into short learning experiences. It is intended to address problems where learners can recall information but fail to perform in real situations due to [[Interference]] or freezing under pressure.

## Steps
The procedure consists of seven sequential steps:
1.  **Choose one target rung** from the [[Outcome Ladder]] (A, B, C, or D).
2.  **Write one observable pass condition** that you can see or measure.
3.  **Create one short scenario** that includes a constraint, such as time pressure or an annoyed colleague.
4.  **Offer two actions**: the intended correct action and a tempting wrong action.
5.  **Ask for a choice and a one-sentence reason** for that choice.
6.  **Give feedback in two layers**: a short [[Rule Of Thumb]] (fast layer) followed by a deeper explanation (slow layer).
7.  **Add a "next repetition" prompt** specifying when and how to repeat the practice.

## Failure modes
Common mistakes associated with this design process include:
-   **Targeting the wrong rung** or mixing multiple skills in one unit, which increases [[Friction Budget]] without improving [[Transfer (Learning)]].
-   **Adding excessive content** (examples, theory) under the misconception that "more content means more learning."
-   Assuming that recognition equals performance, ignoring that [[Transfer (Learning)]] requires spaced repetition and practice under constraints.
