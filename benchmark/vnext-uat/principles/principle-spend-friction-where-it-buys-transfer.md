# Spend Friction Where It Buys Transfer

## Rule
When designing learning, do not indiscriminately reduce all difficulty. Instead, strategically allocate a learner's limited [[Friction Budget]] towards challenges that directly build the ability to apply skills in real-world, high-pressure situations ([[Transfer]]), as opposed to friction that merely adds content or complexity.

## Rationale
Every learning unit has a finite [[Friction Budget]]; exceeding it causes learners to quit or guess. The goal is not to eliminate friction but to invest it where it yields a return in higher-level [[Outcome Ladder]] rungs, particularly [[Performance]] and [[Transfer]]. Adding unnecessary content or theory raises friction without improving the learner's ability to act later under constraints like stress or time pressure.
