# Interference As A Learning Barrier

## Rule
When designing learning, prioritize mitigating [[Interference]]—the phenomenon where learners freeze under pressure, forget steps, or misuse tools—over simply adding more content or assuming the problem is a lack of motivation. Spend the [[Friction Budget]] strategically to build [[Transfer (Learning)]] by practicing under realistic constraints.

## Rationale
Interference causes learners to fail in real situations despite having "understood" the material, because [[Recognition Is Not Performance]]. To overcome interference, learning design must target higher rungs on the [[Outcome Ladder]] (like Perform or Transfer) using methods like the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]] with its [[Two-Speed Feedback]] and [[Calibration Loop]]. This approach builds robust skills that work later, in different contexts, and under stress.
