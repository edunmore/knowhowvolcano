# Friction Budget

## Rule
Every learning unit has a finite "friction budget," which is the maximum mental effort a learner can expend before they quit or resort to superficial guessing. This budget must be spent deliberately to buy transfer, not on excessive content or complexity.

## Rationale
Friction accumulates when a unit requires too many new terms at once, too many steps, or too much switching between contexts. Exceeding the budget causes learners to abandon deliberate practice and default to passive reading, which fails to build the targeted skill. The principle emphasizes strategic allocation of cognitive load, not mere simplification.
