# Outcome Ladder

## Definition
The Outcome Ladder is a framework that separates learning outcomes into four distinct rungs, each representing a different level of capability. It is used to clarify what real-world success looks like for a learner, moving from basic recall to complex application.

## Operationalization
The ladder is operationalized as four sequential rungs: A) remember (can recall terms), B) recognize (can pick the right option from examples), C) perform (can do the behavior in a realistic scenario), and D) transfer (can do it later, in a different context, under stress). A specific outcome is targeted by choosing one of these rungs and writing an observable pass condition that can be seen or measured.

## Boundary conditions
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) Does the model apply to all types of skills or knowledge domains? (2) Are the rungs strictly hierarchical, or can some outcomes bypass lower levels?
