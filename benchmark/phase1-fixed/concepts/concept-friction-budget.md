# Friction Budget

## Definition
The Friction Budget is the maximum amount of mental effort a learner is willing or able to expend on a learning unit before disengaging or resorting to superficial strategies like guessing. Exceeding this budget causes learners to abandon [[deliberate practice]].

## Key Components
The source describes factors that consume the budget, which can be abstracted as types of instructional friction:
- **Terminological Load**: Introducing too many new terms or concepts at once.
- **Procedural Complexity**: Requiring too many steps or cognitive operations.
- **Interface Switching**: Demanding excessive navigation or context shifts between screens or materials.

## Application
In instructional design, the Friction Budget is managed by strategically allocating mental effort towards skills that lead to transfer (Level 4/Transfer on the [[Outcome Ladder]]), rather than eliminating all difficulty. Designers must balance complexity to stay within the learner's tolerance to sustain engagement with practice tasks.
