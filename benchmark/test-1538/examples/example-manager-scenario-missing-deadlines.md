# Manager Scenario: Missing Deadlines

## Scenario
A manager is presented with a scenario where a team member says, “I keep missing deadlines because other teams block me.” The manager must choose between two actions: one that involves immediate advice and one that involves exploring a concrete example first.

## Context
This scenario is presented as a sample for designing [[Microlearning]] units. The context for the learning unit is the common real-world challenge of [[Interference (Learning)]]—where managers freeze under pressure or choose the wrong tool. The scenario is designed with a constraint (an external blocker) to practice applying a skill in a realistic context.

## Key Insight
This example illustrates the application of the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]] for teaching [[Transfer (Learning Outcome)]] (Rung D). It demonstrates how a realistic scenario with a constraint is used to test a learner’s ability to apply a [[Rule of Thumb]] under pressure, moving beyond mere [[Recognition (Learning Outcome)]] to [[Performance (Learning Outcome)]].

## Structure
- **Setup:** A team member reports a recurring problem with missed deadlines, blaming other teams.
- **Tension:** The manager must decide how to respond in the moment.
- **Resolution:** The learner must choose between two provided actions (Action A or Action B) and give a one-sentence reason.
- **Outcome:** The example is structured to facilitate a [[Calibration Loop]], where the learner commits to a decision before receiving [[Two-Speed Feedback]] that names the hidden variables (like exploring constraints vs. giving immediate advice).

## Rewrite Opportunity
The core, transferable pattern is a binary-choice scenario with a common workplace friction point (an employee presenting a systemic problem).
- **Extractable Pattern:** A scenario where the learner must choose between a diagnostic/exploratory action and a prescriptive/judgmental action.
- **Transferable Elements:** The role (manager), the type of problem (recurring blame), the constraint (time pressure of a live conversation), and the tempting wrong action (immediate advice) can be adapted to other domains like customer service or project management.
