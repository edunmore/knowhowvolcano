# Spend Friction Where It Buys Transfer

## Rule
When designing learning, do not minimize friction indiscriminately. Instead, strategically allocate a learner's limited [[Friction Budget]] towards activities that directly contribute to [[Transfer (Outcome Ladder)]]—the ability to perform a skill later, in a different context, and under stress.

## Rationale
Every learning unit has a finite [[Friction Budget]]; exceeding it causes learners to quit or engage in superficial reading. The goal is not ease, but effective investment. Friction spent on activities that only build [[Recall (Outcome Ladder)]] or [[Recognition (Outcome Ladder)]] yields less real-world performance. Therefore, friction should be reserved for deliberate practice elements—like the constrained scenarios, choice commitments, and [[Two-Speed Feedback]] in the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]]—that are proven to build durable, applicable skill.
