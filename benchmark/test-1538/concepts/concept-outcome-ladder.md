# Outcome Ladder

## Definition
The Outcome Ladder is a framework that separates learning outcomes into four ascending "rungs" of capability, from simple recall to complex application under stress. It is a tool used to define what real-world success looks like for a learner and to avoid the common mistake of measuring only low-level recall.

## Operationalization
The four rungs are explicitly defined:
- **Rung A: Remember** – The learner can recall terms or facts.
- **Rung B: Recognize** – The learner can pick the correct option from examples.
- **Rung C: Perform** – The learner can execute the behavior in a realistic scenario.
- **Rung D: Transfer** – The learner can apply the skill later, in a different context, and under stress (e.g., pressure, interference).
The ladder is operationalized by selecting a target rung (A, B, C, or D) and writing an "observable pass condition" that can be seen or measured.

## Boundary conditions
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) Is the ladder intended only for skill-based learning, or does it apply to knowledge/attitude outcomes? (2) Are the rungs strictly sequential, or can a learner achieve transfer (Rung D) without mastering a lower rung in a specific instance?
