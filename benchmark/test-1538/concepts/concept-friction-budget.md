# Friction Budget

## Definition
The Friction Budget is a design constraint for a learning unit, representing the maximum amount of mental effort a learner can expend before they disengage or resort to superficial strategies like guessing. It is a finite resource that is consumed when a unit introduces too many new terms, steps, or context switches.

## Operationalization
Friction can be observed when a learner quits an activity or starts guessing. It grows measurably when a learning unit requires "too many new terms at once, too many steps, or too much switching between screens." Exceeding the budget results in learners abandoning [[Deliberate Practice]] and defaulting to superficial reading.

## Boundary Conditions
The concept applies during the design and consumption of a [[Microlearning]] unit. It does not mean making everything easy; instead, friction should be strategically "spent where it buys [[Transfer]]." Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) How is the specific budget quantified or estimated for a given learner? (2) Does the budget vary significantly between different [[Outcome Ladder]] rungs?
