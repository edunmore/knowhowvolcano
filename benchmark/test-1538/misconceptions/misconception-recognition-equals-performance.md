# Recognition Equals Performance

## Misconception
The mistaken belief that a learner's ability to recognize or understand a correct model or answer ([[Rung B: Recognize]]) is equivalent to their ability to reliably execute the correct behavior in a realistic or stressful situation ([[Rung C: Perform]]).

## Why it happens
The source text suggests this misconception arises because learning designers often test only for recall or recognition ([[Rung A: Remember]] or [[Rung B: Recognize]]) and then "celebrate progress while performance stays flat." There is an implied assumption that if learners understand something once, they can automatically apply it later.

## Correction
[[Recognition]] is not [[Performance]]. True learning requires designing for [[Transfer (Learning)]] ([[Rung D: Transfer]]), which involves "repetition spaced over time and practice under constraints." Learning units should target higher rungs on the [[Outcome Ladder]] and include realistic scenarios with constraints like [[Time Pressure]] to bridge the gap between recognition and performance.
