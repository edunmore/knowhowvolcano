# Friction Budget

## Definition
The Friction Budget is a concept in [[Instructional Design]] representing the maximum amount of mental effort a learner can spend on a learning unit before they quit or start guessing. It is a finite resource that is consumed when a unit requires too many new terms, steps, or context switches. The goal is not to eliminate friction, but to spend it strategically to buy [[Transfer]].

## Operationalization
Friction can be observed and managed by analyzing the demands of a learning unit. It grows when the unit requires "too many new terms at once, too many steps, or too much switching between screens." When the friction budget is exceeded, learners stop [[Deliberate Practice]] and default to superficial reading. Designers can operationalize this by simplifying units to focus on a single [[Outcome Ladder]] rung and by using the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]].

## Boundary conditions
The concept applies to the design of learning units, particularly [[Microlearning]]. It may not apply or may need adaptation for expert learners who get bored easily, as noted in an open question in the source. The toolbox it is part of "works best when the skill can be observed" and "can fail when outcomes are invisible or when the environment blocks practice."
