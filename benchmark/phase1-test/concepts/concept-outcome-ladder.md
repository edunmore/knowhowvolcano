# Outcome Ladder

## Definition
The Outcome Ladder is a framework for classifying learning outcomes into four distinct levels of mastery, or "rungs." It separates what a learner can recall in theory from what they can do in practice, especially under real-world pressure.

## Operationalization
The four rungs are operationally defined as:
*   **Rung A: Remember** – The learner can recall terms and definitions.
*   **Rung B: Recognize** – The learner can pick the correct option from given examples.
*   **Rung C: Perform** – The learner can execute the behavior in a realistic practice scenario.
*   **Rung D: Transfer** – The learner can apply the skill later, in a different context, and under stress.

A key operational step in using the ladder is to "choose one Outcome Ladder rung as the target" when designing a learning unit and to write one "observable pass condition" for it.

## Boundary conditions
The ladder is presented as a tool for designing [[Microlearning]] units. Its effectiveness is tied to skills that can be observed, even roughly. According to the source, it "can fail when outcomes are invisible or when the environment blocks practice (for example: no permission to try, high risk, or strict scripts)."
