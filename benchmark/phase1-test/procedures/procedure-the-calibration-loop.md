# The Calibration Loop

## When to use
Use this procedure when a learner needs to improve their judgment by comparing their decisions to an expert's. It is implied as a core component of the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]], specifically within the feedback phase after a learner makes a choice. It is designed to accelerate learning from scenarios.

## Steps
1.  **Present a scenario with a decision point.** The learner is given a short scenario that includes a constraint (e.g., time pressure, an annoyed colleague).
2.  **Require a committed choice.** The learner must commit to a specific action (e.g., choose between two provided options) *before* seeing the expert answer.
3.  **Provide expert comparison and feedback.** Show the learner the expert's choice. The feedback must name the "hidden variable" (e.g., risk, trust, time, power, or uncertainty) that explains the difference between the learner's choice and the optimal one.
4.  **Structure feedback in two layers.** Deliver a fast, memorable [[Rule of Thumb]] first, followed by a slower, deeper explanation of why the rule works and when it might fail (i.e., [[Two-Speed Feedback]]).

## Failure modes
- **Skipping the commitment step:** If the learner sees the answer before committing to their own choice, calibration improves more slowly.
- **Vague feedback:** Feedback that does not name the specific "hidden variable" responsible for the performance gap will be less effective.
- **Misapplication:** The procedure works best for skills that can be observed. It can fail when outcomes are invisible or when the environment blocks practice (e.g., no permission to try, high risk, or strict scripts).
