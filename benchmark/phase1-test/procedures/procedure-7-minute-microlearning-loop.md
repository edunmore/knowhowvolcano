# 7-Minute Microlearning Loop

## When to use
Use this procedure to design a single [[Microlearning]] unit that aims to turn internal know-how into a short, effective learning experience. It is best applied when the target skill can be observed, even roughly, and the environment permits practice.

## Steps
1.  **Choose one target rung.** Select a single rung from the [[Outcome Ladder]] (A: remember, B: recognize, C: perform, D: transfer) as the unit's goal.
2.  **Write one observable pass condition.** Define a single, measurable action or output that indicates success.
3.  **Create one short scenario with a constraint.** Design a realistic situation that includes a constraint like time pressure, an annoyed colleague, or missing data.
4.  **Offer two actions.** Present the learner with the intended correct action and one tempting wrong action.
5.  **Ask for choice and reason.** Require the learner to select an action *and* provide a one-sentence justification for their choice.
6.  **Give [[Two-Speed Feedback]].** Provide feedback in two layers: first a short, memorable [[Rule of Thumb]], then a deeper explanation of why it works and when it fails.
7.  **Add a "next repetition" prompt.** Instruct the learner when and how to repeat the practice (e.g., tomorrow, in the next meeting, or on the next real case).

## Failure modes
-   **Targeting the wrong rung.** Designing a unit that tests recall (Rung A) while expecting real-world performance (Rung C or D) leads to a false sense of progress.
-   **Exceeding the [[Friction Budget]].** Including too many new terms, steps, or requiring excessive screen-switching can cause learners to quit deliberate practice.
-   **Adding unnecessary content.** Including extra examples or theory raises friction without improving [[Transfer]].
-   **Unobservable outcomes.** The procedure can fail when the skill's outcomes are invisible or when the environment blocks practice (e.g., no permission to try, high risk, strict scripts).
