# Recognition Equals Performance

## Misconception
The mistaken belief that if a learner can recognize or understand a correct model in a learning context, they will be able to perform the associated behavior later in a real-world situation. This assumption leads to celebrating progress on lower [[Outcome Ladder]] rungs while performance remains flat.

## Why it happens
The source text does not explicitly state the causes of this misconception. Open questions: (1) Does it stem from over-reliance on simple [[Recall]]-based testing? (2) Is it a failure to account for [[Interference Under Stress]]?

## Correction
[[Recognition]] is not [[Performance]]. True [[Transfer]] of a skill requires deliberate practice under realistic [[Constraints]] (like time pressure or an annoyed colleague) and [[Spaced Repetition]] over time, not just one-time understanding. Learning design must target higher rungs of the [[Outcome Ladder]] (C: Perform, D: Transfer) to bridge this gap.
