# Manager Scenario: Missed Deadlines

## Scenario
A manager is approached by a team member who says, “I keep missing deadlines because other teams block me.” The manager must choose between two actions: one is to ask for a concrete example and explore constraints before proposing a solution (Action A), and the other is to give immediate advice about communication and boundaries (Action B). The scenario is presented as a sample for a [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]].

## Context
The situation is a typical managerial conversation where a direct report is presenting a recurring performance problem (missed deadlines) and attributing the cause externally (other teams). The scenario is designed as a learning unit, likely incorporating a constraint to simulate pressure, though the specific constraint here is implied by the real-world tension of providing helpful versus hasty advice.

## Key Insight
This example illustrates the common managerial mistake of skipping diagnostic inquiry in favor of immediate, prescriptive solutions. It teaches that the tempting, "helpful" action (B) can fail because it may trigger defensiveness and overlook the root cause, while the better action (A) aligns with a coaching approach that first seeks to understand the specific context.

## Structure
- **Setup:** A team member reports a pattern of missed deadlines due to being blocked by other teams.
- **Tension:** The manager must choose between a diagnostic, inquiry-based response and a direct, advice-giving response.
- **Resolution:** The source notes that many new managers choose the immediate advice (Action B), which is the tempting wrong action in the learning design.
- **Outcome:** The example demonstrates that Action B often fails because it skips the diagnostic step, highlighting the gap between recognition of good practice and actual [[Performance (Outcome Ladder)]] under pressure.

## Rewrite Opportunity
- **Core Pattern:** A direct report presents a problem with an external attribution; the leader's choice is between inquiry (diagnosis) and declaration (prescription).
- **Transferable Elements:** This pattern applies to any coaching or mentoring situation where the learner (the manager) must resist the urge to solve and instead practice [[Diagnostic Inquiry]].
