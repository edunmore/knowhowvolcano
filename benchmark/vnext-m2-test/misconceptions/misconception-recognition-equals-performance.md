# Recognition Equals Performance

## Misconception
The wrong belief is that if a learner understands or recognizes a correct model or principle, they will be able to perform the associated skill when it is needed. This manifests as assuming that comprehension in a learning session guarantees future application, especially under pressure.

## Why it happens
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) Why do instructors or learners conflate understanding with performance capability? (2) What systemic or psychological factors reinforce this belief in educational or training contexts?

## Correction
The correction is that [[Recognition]] is not [[Performance]]. True ability, or [[Transfer]], requires deliberate practice under constraints and [[Spaced Repetition]] over time. Effective learning design must target the correct rung of the [[Outcome Ladder]] (particularly Rung D: Transfer) and include practice scenarios with elements like time pressure or stress.
