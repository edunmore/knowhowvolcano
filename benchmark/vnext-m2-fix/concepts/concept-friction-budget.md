# Friction Budget

## Definition
The **Friction Budget** is a concept in learning design representing the maximum amount of mental effort a learner can expend on a learning unit before they disengage or resort to superficial strategies like guessing. It is a finite resource that must be managed by instructional designers.

## Operationalization
Friction is observed to increase when a learning unit requires too many new [[Terminology|terms]] at once, presents too many steps in a sequence, or demands excessive switching between different screens or contexts. A budget is exceeded when learners stop engaging in [[Deliberate Practice]] and default to passive reading.

## Boundary conditions
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) Is the friction budget a static, universal limit, or does it vary per learner or context? (2) How does the budget interact with learner motivation or prior knowledge?
