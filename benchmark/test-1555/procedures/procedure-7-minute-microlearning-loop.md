# 7-Minute Microlearning Loop

## When to use
Use this loop to design a single [[Microlearning]] unit, especially when the goal is to turn internal know-how into short learning that sticks and transfers to real-world performance.

## Steps
1. **Choose one [[Outcome Ladder]] rung as the target** (A, B, C, or D).
2. **Write one “observable pass condition”** (what you can see or measure).
3. **Create one short scenario with a constraint** (e.g., time pressure, an annoyed colleague, missing data).
4. **Offer two actions**: the intended action and a tempting wrong action.
5. **Ask for a choice *and* a one-sentence reason**.
6. **Give feedback in two layers**: first a short [[Rule of Thumb]], then a deeper explanation.
7. **Add a “next repetition” prompt**: when and how to repeat (e.g., tomorrow, next meeting, or next real case).

## Failure modes
- **Targeting the wrong [[Outcome Ladder]] rung**: Celebrating progress on recall (Rung A) while real performance (Rung C/D) stays flat.
- **Exceeding the [[Friction Budget]]**: Adding too many new terms, steps, or switching contexts, causing learners to quit or guess.
- **Mixing multiple skills in one unit**: Adding extra content or theory that raises friction without improving [[Transfer]].
- **Assuming recognition equals performance**: Believing that if a learner understands a model once, they will perform it later under stress.
