# Manager Scenario: Missed Deadlines

## Scenario
You are a manager. A team member approaches you and says, "I keep missing deadlines because other teams block me." You are presented with two possible actions: (A) Ask for a concrete recent example and explore constraints before proposing a solution, or (B) Give advice immediately: "Just communicate earlier and set boundaries."

## Context
The scenario is presented as a sample for a [[Calibration Loop]] exercise. The learner is expected to choose between two actions and receive [[Two-Speed Feedback]]. The context implies a typical workplace pressure where a manager must respond helpfully, but there is a risk of skipping diagnosis.

## Key Insight
This example illustrates the common managerial mistake of jumping to solutions without first diagnosing the problem, which can skip a critical [[Diagnostic Step]] and trigger defensiveness. It teaches that the [[Intended Action]] (A) is to explore a concrete example and constraints, while the [[Tempting Wrong Action]] (B) feels helpful but is often ineffective.

## Structure
- **Setup:** A team member reports a recurring problem (missed deadlines) and blames other teams.
- **Tension:** The manager must choose between a diagnostic, exploratory action and a quick, prescriptive one.
- **Resolution:** The source indicates that many new managers choose the quick advice (B), but the intended action is the diagnostic approach (A).
- **Outcome:** Choosing B often fails because it skips diagnosis and can trigger defensiveness, demonstrating the gap between [[Recognition And Performance]].

## Rewrite Opportunity
- **Core Pattern:** A scenario where a subordinate presents a problem with an apparent cause, and the leader must resist the urge to give immediate advice in favor of a diagnostic inquiry.
- **Transferable Elements:** The structure of presenting two contrasting actions (one diagnostic, one prescriptive) under a common constraint (like time pressure or an annoyed colleague) can be applied to teaching other skills like [[Coaching]], [[Problem Solving]], or [[Giving Feedback]].
