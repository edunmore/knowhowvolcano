# Friction Budget

## Definition
The **Friction Budget** is a concept in [[Microlearning]] design representing the maximum mental effort a learner can expend on a learning unit before they disengage or resort to superficial strategies like guessing. It is a finite resource that is consumed by factors like introducing too many new terms, complex steps, or excessive screen-switching. The goal is not to eliminate friction, but to strategically allocate it where it best enables [[Transfer (Learning)]] to real-world performance.

## Operationalization
Friction is observed when learners "stop doing [[Deliberate Practice]] and default to superficial reading." It can be identified by monitoring learner engagement and performance. Designers can measure potential friction by auditing a learning unit for the number of new concepts, procedural steps, and context switches required, ensuring the total does not exceed the estimated learner capacity for that unit.

## Boundary conditions
The concept applies specifically to the design of [[Microlearning]] units intended to build performance. It is not about making learning "easy," but about budgeting cognitive load effectively. A key boundary condition is that friction is acceptable and should be spent if it directly "buys transfer."
