# Spend Friction Where It Buys Transfer

## Rule
When designing learning, do not seek to minimize all mental effort. Instead, strategically allocate the learner's limited [[Friction Budget]] towards activities that directly build [[Transfer (Outcome)]] (Rung D on the [[Outcome Ladder]]), such as practicing under realistic [[Constraint]]s and receiving [[Two-Speed Feedback]].

## Rationale
Every learning unit has a finite [[Friction Budget]]; exceeding it causes learners to quit or guess. The goal is not ease, but efficacy. Friction spent on activities that only build recall (Rung A) or recognition (Rung B) does not buy [[Transfer (Outcome)]]. Transfer requires overcoming [[Interference]] from stress and context change, which is only achieved through deliberate practice within the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]] and the [[Calibration Loop]]. Adding extra content or theory without this focus raises friction without improving transfer.
