# More Content Means More Learning

## Misconception
The belief that adding more examples, explanations, or theoretical content to a learning unit inherently increases the amount of [[Learning Transfer|transfer]] or performance achieved by the learner. This is often seen in teams who think that more content equals more learning.

## Why it happens
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) Is this driven by a desire for comprehensiveness or perceived value? (2) Does it stem from confusing content coverage with skill development?

## Correction
The correction is that extra content often raises [[Friction Budget]] without improving [[Learning Transfer|transfer]]. Effective design focuses on spending friction only where it buys transfer. If a rule of thumb requires lengthy justification, the unit may be targeting the wrong rung of the [[Outcome Ladder]] or mixing multiple skills. The goal is to design efficient units, such as the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]], that target specific, observable outcomes.
