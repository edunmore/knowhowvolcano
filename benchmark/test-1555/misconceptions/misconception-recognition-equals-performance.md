# Recognition Equals Performance

## Misconception
The mistaken belief that a learner's ability to recognize or understand a concept (e.g., nodding at a good model) is equivalent to their ability to perform the associated behavior in a real-world situation, particularly under pressure.

## Why it happens
The Source Text attributes this to a common mistake in learning design: celebrating progress when learners pass tests that only require [[Recognition (Learning Outcome)]] (Rung B) or recall (Rung A) while real-world [[Performance (Learning Outcome)]] remains flat. This leads to an overestimation of a learner's capability.

## Correction
Recognition is not performance. True [[Transfer (Learning Outcome)]] requires deliberate practice, which includes repetition spaced over time and practice under realistic [[Constraints (Scenario Design)]] (e.g., time pressure, stress, or an annoyed colleague). Effective learning design must target higher rungs on the [[Outcome Ladder]] and use tools like the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]] and [[Calibration Loop]] to build performance, not just recognition.
