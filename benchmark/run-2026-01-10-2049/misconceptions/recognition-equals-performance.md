# Recognition Equals Performance

## Misconception
The wrong belief is that if a learner can recognize or understand a concept when presented (e.g., nodding at a good model), they will be able to perform the associated skill later in a real-world situation. This leads to assessing learning only at the recognition stage and celebrating progress while actual performance remains unchanged.

## Correction
Recognition is not performance. True learning requires achieving [[transfer]], which is the ability to perform a behavior later, in a different context, and under pressure or constraints. This requires deliberate practice, repetition spaced over time, and practice under realistic constraints, not just comprehension or recognition.
