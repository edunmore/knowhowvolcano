# Spend Friction Where It Buys Transfer

## Rule
When designing learning experiences, do not aim to minimize all mental effort; instead, strategically allocate difficulty (friction) to points where it directly promotes the ability to apply skills in new, real-world contexts (transfer).

## Rationale
Every learning unit has a limited "friction budget" for mental effort before learners disengage. Exceeding this budget through excessive new terms, steps, or screen-switching causes learners to default to superficial reading instead of deliberate practice. The principle corrects the misconception that "more content means more learning" by stating that extra, unstrategic content raises friction without improving transfer. Friction should be invested in activities that build performance under constraint and spaced repetition, which are necessary for transfer.
