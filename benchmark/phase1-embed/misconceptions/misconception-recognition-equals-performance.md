# Recognition Equals Performance

## Misconception
The wrong belief is that if a learner can understand a concept or recognize a correct answer in a learning context, they will be able to perform the associated skill later in a real-world situation. This assumes that comprehension or recognition automatically leads to competent action.

## Correction
The correct understanding is that recognition is distinct from performance. True skill application (performance) and, especially, [[Transfer]] to new contexts under pressure require deliberate practice, repetition spaced over time, and practice under specific constraints. The [[Outcome Ladder]] framework explicitly separates the ability to recognize (Level 2, author's "Rung B") from the ability to perform (Level 3, author's "Rung C").
