# Outcome Ladder

## Definition
The Outcome Ladder is a model that separates learning outcomes into four ascending levels of proficiency, or "rungs." It is used to clarify what real-world success looks like for a skill, moving from simple recall to the ability to perform under pressure in novel contexts.

## Operationalization
The ladder is operationalized by its four distinct rungs, each with a defined capability: Rung A (remember) is recalling terms; Rung B (recognize) is picking the right option from examples; Rung C (perform) is doing the behavior in a realistic scenario; Rung D (transfer) is doing it later, in a different context, under stress. The target rung for a learning unit is chosen explicitly, and success is measured against an "observable pass condition."

## Boundary conditions
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) Does the model apply to all types of knowledge/skills, or only procedural/interpersonal ones? (2) Are the rungs strictly sequential, or can learners skip levels for certain skills?
