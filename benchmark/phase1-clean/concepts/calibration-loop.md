# Calibration Loop

## Definition
A Calibration Loop is a process where a learner improves their skill by comparing their own decision or action to an expert's choice and understanding the reasoning behind the difference. It is a key mechanism for developing accurate self-assessment and improving performance.

## Key Components
1.  **Commitment to a Decision:** The learner must commit to their own choice before seeing the expert answer.
2.  **Comparison to Expert Choice:** The learner's decision is explicitly compared to the choice an expert would make.
3.  **Identification of Key Variables:** The feedback highlights the critical, often hidden, contextual variable (e.g., risk, trust, time, power, or uncertainty) that explains the difference between the choices.

## Application
This concept is applied within the [[7-Minute Microlearning Loop]] to foster skill transfer. It is especially effective when designing feedback for scenario-based practice, ensuring learners move beyond passive recognition to active, justified decision-making. The loop accelerates learning by directly addressing the gap between the learner's current judgment and expert judgment.
