# Coaching as the Antidote to Command and Control

## Rule
When faced with time pressure or fear, a leader's default is often command and control. However, to foster employee development and improve work quality, a leader should adopt a coaching leadership style instead. This shift is critical for meeting the needs of employees, particularly younger generations seeking meaning, and for achieving long-term performance benefits.

## Rationale
The source text identifies a hierarchy of criteria that influence leadership behavior: time pressure and fear are the top drivers, pushing leaders toward command and control, while the quality of work and the need for employee development demand coaching. A coaching style is presented as the direct counter to a command style; where "telling negates choice, disempowers, limits potential, and demotivates, coaching does the opposite." Adopting coaching is necessary because younger employees seek work with meaning and purpose and will leave if their needs—including for development and a coaching style that builds [[self-belief]]—are not met. Leaders operating at higher levels of [[Maslow's hierarchy]], such as the self-belief or self-actualizing levels, are more likely to exhibit this altruistic, service-oriented leadership.
