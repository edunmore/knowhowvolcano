# Coaching Requires Coaching Expertise, Not Subject Expertise

## Rule
A coach's effectiveness stems from their expertise in the [[coaching]] process itself, not from their knowledge of the coachee's specific subject or field. This allows the coachee to progress beyond the coach's personal experience and knowledge.

## Rationale
Coaching that relies on a more experienced person passing down their knowledge "undermines the building of self-belief which creates sustained performance." Instead, true coaching works "from [the coachee's] experience and perceptions rather than from [the coach's] own." This principle is illustrated by the story of Mike Sprecklen, who, after exhausting his technical rowing knowledge, used coaching methods to help athletes "go further, for they can feel things that I can't even see."
