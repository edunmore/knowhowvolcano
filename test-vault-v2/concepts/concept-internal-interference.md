# Internal Interference

## Definition
Internal Interference, as conceptualized by [[Timothy Gallwey]], refers to the "internal obstacles to performance" or "the opponent within one's own head." It is the psychological or self-imposed barrier that inhibits an individual's natural ability to learn and perform, and it is represented by the variable `i` in the [[Inner Game Equation]] (`P = p - i`). Reducing this interference is a core objective of modern coaching.

## Operationalization
The source text does not provide a concrete method for identifying or measuring internal interference. ## Boundary conditions
The concept is introduced in the context of sports performance (tennis, skiing, golf) but is framed as the foundation for modern coaching, which is broadly about "unlocking people's potential to maximize their own performance." The source does not specify if the nature of interference differs across domains (e.g., creative vs. analytical tasks) or in group/team contexts.
