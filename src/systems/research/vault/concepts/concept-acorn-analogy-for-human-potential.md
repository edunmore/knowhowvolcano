# Acorn Analogy for Human Potential

## Definition
An analogy, implied in the text, that compares a person's latent capabilities to an acorn. Just as an acorn naturally contains the potential to become a full oak tree, a person possesses a built-in, natural potential for growth and learning. The role of coaching is not to instruct or build from the outside, but to provide the right conditions for this inherent potential to unfold and maximize performance.

## Operationalization
Not specified in this source. Open questions: (1) How does the analogy guide a coach's specific actions or communication style? (2) What practical coaching behaviors stem from the belief that potential is inherent and natural, rather than something to be implanted?

## Boundary conditions
The source text contrasts the coaching approach (unlocking inherent potential) with instruction or teaching. The analogy is therefore central to a coaching context where the goal is to help someone learn from within and maximize their own performance, as opposed to a context focused on direct instruction, teaching of specific technical facts, or compliance with external authority.
