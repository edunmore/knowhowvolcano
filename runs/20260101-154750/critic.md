## B) One-Page Business Fable Outline

**Title:** The Manager Who Learned to Ask
**Characters:** Alex (frustrated, directive manager), Sam (disengaged team member), Jordan (wise mentor/coach).
**Setting:** A product team missing launch deadlines.

1.  **The Grind:** Alex berates Sam for another missed milestone, dictating a solution ("You **will** work weekends!"). Sam nods, demoralized. Productivity drops further.
2.  **The Intervention:** Mentor Jordan asks Alex, "What's your **goal** for the team?" Alex states, "To hit the deadline." Jordan probes: "Is that inspiring, or just reacting to panic?"
3.  **A New Path:** Jordan guides Alex through a quiet conversation with Sam.
    *   **G (Goal):** "Sam, what do you want this product launch to achieve for you and our customers?" Sam reveals a desire to build something elegant, not just fast.
    *   **R (Reality):** "What's *actually* slowing you down?" Sam confesses a critical, unclear requirement from another department.
    *   **O (Options):** "What could we do about that?" They brainstorm: escalate, prototype, re-scope.
    *   **W (Will):** "What will you do?" Sam commits to clarifying the requirement by tomorrow. Alex commits to backing them up.
4.  **The Shift:** The blockage clears. Sam is energized, taking ownership. Alex realizes the power of asking, not telling. The team begins using the GROW rhythm informally.
5.  **The Moral:** The framework (GROW) is the map, but awareness, responsibility, and genuine inquiry (the compass) are what guide you to performance.
