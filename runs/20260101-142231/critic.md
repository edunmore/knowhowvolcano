### B) One-Page Business Fable Outline

**Title:** The Manager Who Learned to Listen

**Characters:**
*   **Alex:** A capable but overwhelmed project manager, frustrated with her team.
*   **Sam:** A seasoned leader (the coach).
*   **Team Members (Johann & Catherine):** Seemingly underperforming, disengaged.

**Plot:**
1.  **The Problem (Impulsive/Dependent Stage):** Alex is working late, fixing everyone's work. She complains to Sam that Johann and Catherine are unreliable and the project is at risk. She demands more resources.
2.  **The First Conversation (Goal):** Sam asks, "What do you want from our chat?" and "What's your ideal outcome?" Alex states she wants the team to "step up." Sam guides her to a positive, owned goal: "A team that pulls together to delight the customer."
3.  **The Second Conversation (Reality):** Sam asks, "What's working?" Alex lists other teams performing well. Sam asks, "How did you contribute to that?" and "What's different with Johann and Catherine?" Alex realizes she never properly onboarded them and has been micromanaging based on gossip.
4.  **The Shift (Awareness):** Alex has a "Eureka!" moment. The obstacle isn't her team—it's her approach. She sees her own lack of trust and clarity.
5.  **The Application (Options & Will):** Alex meets Johann and Catherine. She uses descriptive questions to understand their reality, then asks, "What could we do differently?" They co-create a plan with clear actions and dates. Ownership shifts.
6.  **The Result (Interdependent Stage):** The project delivers on time. Alex spends less time firefighting. Johann and Catherine become advocates. Alex learns: true leadership isn't having the answers, it's unlocking them in others.

**Moral:** Performance isn't pushed by pressure; it's unleashed through awareness and owned responsibility.

**Where I got stuck / What was missing:**
*   **Stuck on internal conflict:** The extraction is strong on external dialogue examples but lighter on detailing the coachee's *internal* emotional journey from frustration to insight. I inferred this from the "Eureka!" and "overwhelmed" cues.
*   **Missing nuanced failure:** The fable needs a clear "before" state. The extraction's "Failure Modes" (Autocratic Use, Rushing) are procedural. I had to extrapolate a vivid, emotional starting point from the "Sam & Michelle" dialogue snippets.
