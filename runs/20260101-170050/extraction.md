# Method Kernel

**Purpose**
The GROW model serves as a flexible framework for structured coaching conversations, aiming to improve coachee performance and learning through enhanced awareness and responsibility. The specific "G" (Goal Setting) phase focuses on establishing clear objectives for both the coaching session and broader issues, fostering self-motivation and commitment [EXTRACTED] "framework for coaching" (016..., 9 The GROW Model, p1), "determining a goal... define what they want to get from it." (017..., The goal for the session, p1). The "R" (Reality) phase clarifies the current situation objectively, bringing goals into sharper focus [EXTRACTED] "clarify the current situation" (018..., 11 R: What Is Reality?, p1). The "O" (Options) stage generates a broad list of alternative courses of action without immediate judgment or censorship, stimulating creative thinking [EXTRACTED] "purpose of the options stage... not to find the 'right' answer, but to create and list as many alternative courses of action as possible." (019..., 12 O: What Options Do You Have?, p1).

**Preconditions**
Effective coaching requires a foundation of awareness and responsibility, cultivated through active listening and powerful questions [EXTRACTED] "little value without the context of awareness and responsibility" (016..., More than GROW, p1). For goal setting, coachee ownership is crucial for self-motivation and commitment, and goals should be Specific, Measurable, Agreed, Realistic, Timeframed (SMART), Positively stated, Understood, Relevant, Ethical (PURE), Challenging, Legal, Environmentally sound, Appropriate, Recorded (CLEAR) [EXTRACTED] "denies ownership to those who are expected to meet these targets" (017..., Ownership of goals, p1), "goals need to be SMART" (017..., Qualities of a good goal, p1). In the reality phase, both coach and coachee must strive for detachment and objectivity, recognizing and mitigating internal factors that distort perception [EXTRACTED] "potential distortions of both the coach and the coachee must be bypassed" (018..., Detachment, p1). During option generation, a safe environment is necessary for expressing ideas without inhibition or fear of judgment [EXTRACTED] "create an environment in which participants will feel safe enough to express their thoughts" (019..., Maximizing choices, p1).

**Roles**
The primary roles are the Coach (often a leader) and Coachee (a direct report, peer, student, or even a boss) [EXTRACTED] "between a leader and a direct report" (016..., One to one, p1). The coach's role involves guiding the coachee through the GROW sequence, asking powerful questions, and fostering awareness and responsibility. The coachee's role involves defining goals, exploring reality, generating options, and committing to action, maintaining ownership throughout. **Process**
The GROW model follows four distinct stages: Goal, Reality, Options, and Will, generally in sequence [EXTRACTED] "sequence of questions I suggest follows four distinct stages" (016..., A framework for coaching, p1).
1.  **Goal Setting (G):** Begins with determining a goal for the session, then distinguishing between End Goals (vision), Performance Goals (measurable, within control), Dream Goals (inspiration), and Process Goals (steps). Goals must be positively stated, inspirational, challenging, ethical, and agreed upon by all parties [EXTRACTED] "distinguish end goals from performance goals" (017..., The goal for the issue, p1), "very important to state goals in the positive" (017..., Positive focus, p1).
2.  **Reality Checking (R):** Objectively clarifies the current situation. Focus on descriptive, not judgmental, terminology. Use "what," "when," "where," "who," and "how much" questions to elicit facts. Encourage deeper awareness by probing consciousness and exploring both external (organizational) and internal (thoughts, feelings) realities. Investigate past actions and their effects [EXTRACTED] "descriptive terminology rather than evaluative terminology" (018..., Description not judgment, p1), "initiated by the interrogatives 'what,' 'when,' 'where,' 'who,' and 'how much.'" (018..., Reality questions, p2).
3.  **Generating Options (O):** Create a broad list of alternative actions, prioritizing quantity over quality initially. Combat negative assumptions with "What if...?" questions. Expand creativity by shifting perspectives or using metaphors. The coach can offer ideas without attachment once the coachee has exhausted their own. Options should be listed randomly to avoid subconscious hierarchy [EXTRACTED] "quantity of options is more important at this stage" (019..., 12 O: What Options Do You Have?, p1), "What if there were no obstacles, what would you do then?" (019..., Negative assumptions, p2), "subconscious hierarchy... can be avoided by writing them randomly" (019..., MAPPING THE OPTIONS, p1).

**Decision Rules**
*   When a team proposes a lower goal, the leader should first consider coaching them upward to maintain motivation rather than imposing a higher figure [EXTRACTED] "leader should consider the consequences very carefully before overriding their figure" (017..., WHOSE GOAL?, p1).

*   When gathering options, prioritize quantity over quality and feasibility to stimulate creative thinking [EXTRACTED] "quantity of options is more important at this stage than the quality and feasibility" (019..., 12 O: What Options Do You Have?, p1).

**Failure Modes & Fixes**
*   **Failure:** Autocratic application of GROW without questions, leading to no awareness or choice. **Fix:** Ensure questions are asked to foster awareness and responsibility; avoid imposing solutions [EXTRACTED] "Any dictator can use the GROW model. ...not asked a single question." (016..., More than GROW, p3).
*   **Failure:** Lack of goal ownership, often due to leader-imposed goals. **Fix:** Coach for ownership by involving coachees in defining how goals are met or safety rules are created; ensure goals are agreed upon by all parties [EXTRACTED] "denies ownership to those who are expected to meet these targets, and their performance is likely to suffer" (017..., Ownership of goals, p1), "possible to coach for ownership" (017..., COACHING FOR OWNERSHIP, p1).
*   **Failure:** Negatively stated goals or goals based on fear. **Fix:** Frame goals positively and encourage aiming high to be inspirational and challenging [EXTRACTED] "If you fear failure, you are focused on failure" (017..., Inspiring goals, p3), "very important to state goals in the positive" (017..., Positive focus, p1).
*   **Failure:** Lack of objectivity, detachment, or use of judgmental language in the Reality phase. **Fix:** Strive for detachment, use descriptive language, and ask factual questions ("what," "when," "where," "who," "how much") [EXTRACTED] "distortions caused by the opinions, judgments, expectations" (018..., Objectivity, p1), "descriptive not judgmental" (018..., Reality questions, p1).
*   **Failure:** Premature censorship or negative assumptions limiting option generation. **Fix:** Create a safe environment, prioritize quantity, and use "What if...?" questions to bypass rational censorship [EXTRACTED] "If preferences, censorship, ridicule... are expressed... valuable contributions will be missed" (019..., 12 O: What Options Do You Have?, p1), "What if there were no obstacles, what would you do then?"
