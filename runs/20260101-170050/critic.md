**Title:** The Architect's Dilemma
**Characters:**
*   **Master Elara (Coach):** Wise, observant, asks probing questions. Embodies the principles of the GROW model. (From Fable Spec)
*   **Young Builder Kael (Coachee):** Eager but overwhelmed, prone to immediate problem-solving, initially constrained by self-imposed limits. (From Fable Spec)
*   **The Ancient City (Setting):** A complex, sprawling metropolis symbolizing a challenging project or organization. (From Fable Spec)

**Plot (Expanded from EXTRACTION):**

**Setup:** Kael is tasked with building a new, essential bridge in the Ancient City. He's overwhelmed by the city's chaotic growth, "false reality syndrome" from previous quick fixes, and the "impossibility" of the task. He's operating in a "blame culture" which discourages honest assessment, leading to self-limiting assumptions. His initial attempts are reactive, focusing on "widening a road" rather than a visionary solution. Master Elara, known for fostering "awareness and responsibility," approaches him.

**Inciting Incident (G - Goal):** Elara challenges Kael's problem-driven mindset. Instead of immediately fixing the current "traffic congestion," she asks, "What kind of bridge do you dream of, not just the one you 'have to' build?" (Key Phrase: "Dream Big"). Kael, initially hesitant, describes a majestic, long-term vision—an "inspirational and stretching" end goal for connecting the city's districts, not just alleviating a choke point. Elara ensures Kael "owns" this goal, rather than having it "imposed."

**Rising Action (R - Reality):** Elara then guides Kael to observe the city with "objectivity" and "detachment." She emphasizes "description not judgment," helping Kael overcome his "distortions caused by opinions, judgments, expectations." Using "what, when, where, who, and how much" questions (Question Patterns), she probes for "high-resolution focus." Kael meticulously notes existing structures, traffic flows, and challenges. He realizes some areas are stable, contrary to his initial "overwhelmed" feeling, leading to "deeper awareness" from his "inner filing cabinet." He identifies internal factors distorting his perception. This thorough investigation reveals a clearer picture, making his "goals into sharper focus" and even suggesting "early resolution" for some minor issues. Elara resists rushing to options.

**Climax (O - Options):** When Kael despairs of solutions, held back by "negative assumptions" like "It can't be done," Elara presents a simple puzzle (similar to the "nine-dot exercise" from the extraction, but with city blocks). She then asks, "If you were the city's founder, with limitless resources, how would you connect these lands?" and "What if there were no obstacles, what would you do then?" (Key Phrase: "What if...?"). She encourages Kael to "just come up with one more" idea. Kael, free from censorship and fear of judgment, generates a "large quantity of options" without premature evaluation. He explores "perspective-shifting," imagining solutions as the "CEO." He realizes his "self-limiting assumptions" were the true obstacle, not the city itself. Elara helps Kael sort these, noting "benefits and costs."

**Falling Action (W - Will):** Elara helps Kael select the most viable and inspiring options, ensuring they align with his "Dream Goal." The commitment is born from Kael's newfound "awareness and responsibility," fostering his "self-motivation." Kael, empowered, begins to outline the "Process Goals" (steps) necessary to achieve his performance and end goals. He embraces the challenge, recognizing it as "inspirational and stretching."

**Resolution:** Kael, now a confident leader, starts building the bridge, not just as a structure, but as a testament to transformative leadership. He applies the GROW principles, fostering ownership and objective reality checks within his own team, avoiding "autocratic application" and "leader-imposed goals." The city thrives, a monument to a new way of building—and leading.

**Moral/Lesson:** True solutions emerge not from immediate problem-solving, but from a structured journey: first envisioning the ideal, then clearly seeing what is, and finally, creatively exploring all that could be, unbound by initial assumptions. The greatest obstacles are often those we build ourselves. (From Fable Spec)

**Where I got stuck / What was missing:**
*   **Fable-specific details for "Will":** Similar to the reel script, the "Will" stage's lack of explicit detail in the `Method Kernel` section meant I had to infer how Kael's commitment would manifest in the fable's narrative, beyond a generic "commit to action." I relied heavily on the "Moral/Lesson" and the general coaching context.
*   **Concrete "What if" scenario:** While the extraction mentions "What if there were no obstacles" and "What if you were the CEO," creating a specific, compelling "what if" scenario for Kael to break his assumptions felt slightly generic without more examples in the "Reuse Pack" that were directly fable-ready. I adapted the "Nine dot exercise" concept but it still felt like a metaphor for a metaphor.
*   **Specific examples of "Internal factors that distort perception" or "external realities":** The general statements are there, but for a fable, specific, illustrative examples would have made Kael's realization more vivid without requiring me to invent external lore.
